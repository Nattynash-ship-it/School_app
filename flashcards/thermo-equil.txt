What is the criterion for chemical equilibrium at fixed T and P?	The total Gibbs function G = H − TS of the mixture is a minimum: (dG)_T,P = 0
Why is Gibbs function, not entropy, the criterion at fixed T and P?	The system exchanges heat with its surroundings, so it is not isolated; combining the first and second laws at constant T and P gives dG ≤ 0
Define KP for the ideal-gas reaction νA A + νB B ⇌ νC C + νD D in terms of partial pressures	KP = (P_C^νC · P_D^νD)/(P_A^νA · P_B^νB), all partial pressures in atm
How is KP related to the standard-state Gibbs function change ΔG*(T)?	KP = exp[−ΔG*(T)/(R_u T)], with ΔG* = Σ ν_P ḡ*_P − Σ ν_R ḡ*_R at 1 atm and temperature T
Write KP in terms of mole numbers, total pressure and total moles	KP = (N_C^νC N_D^νD)/(N_A^νA N_B^νB) × (P/N_total)^Δν, Δν = νC + νD − νA − νB, P in atm
What does KP depend on?	Temperature only; not pressure, not inert gases, not initial amounts
KP for a reaction written in reverse?	1/KP of the forward reaction (ln KP changes sign)
KP when all stoichiometric coefficients are doubled?	KP squared (ln KP doubles)
Which pressure unit must be used in KP calculations, and why?	Atmospheres, because the tabulated KP uses a 1 atm standard state in ḡ_i = ḡ_i* + R_u T ln P_i
What does N_total include in the (P/N_total)^Δν factor?	Every kmol present at equilibrium: products, unconverted reactants and inert gases such as N2
Effect of raising temperature on a dissociation reaction?	KP increases (endothermic, hR > 0), so more dissociation
Effect of raising pressure on an equilibrium with Δν > 0?	Shifts toward fewer moles: less dissociation; KP itself is unchanged
Effect of pressure on ½N2 + ½O2 ⇌ NO?	None: Δν = 0, so (P/N_total)^0 = 1
Effect of adding an inert gas at constant total pressure on a Δν > 0 reaction?	More dissociation: N_total rises, which acts like lowering the pressure
How many KP relations are needed for simultaneous reactions?	Number of species minus number of elements; the element balances supply the rest
Rule of thumb: when is a reaction essentially complete or essentially absent?	ln KP above about +7 (KP > 1,000): complete; ln KP below about −7 (KP < 0.001): does not occur
ln KP for H2O ⇌ H2 + ½O2 at 2,000 K (Cengel Table A-28)?	−8.145 (KP ≈ 2.9 × 10⁻⁴)
ln KP for CO2 ⇌ CO + ½O2 at 2,600 K (Cengel Table A-28)?	−2.801, so KP = 0.0608 and KP for CO + ½O2 ⇌ CO2 is 16.46
Van't Hoff equation, differential form	d(ln KP)/dT = hR(T)/(R_u T²)
Van't Hoff equation, integrated form	ln(KP2/KP1) ≈ (hR/R_u)(1/T1 − 1/T2), hR taken constant over the interval
What is hR, the enthalpy of reaction?	Enthalpy of products minus reactants at the same temperature, per kmol of reaction as written; positive for dissociation, negative for combustion
Sign rule from van't Hoff: KP rises with T means?	hR > 0, the reaction is endothermic
Enthalpy of reaction for H2 + ½O2 → H2O near 2,000 K from KP at 1,800 and 2,200 K?	About −251,700 kJ/kmol (tables give −251,663 kJ/kmol)
Define the degree of dissociation α	Fraction of the original kmol of a compound that has split at equilibrium; from 1 kmol of X2: (1 − α) X2, 2α X, N_total = 1 + α
KP for X2 ⇌ 2X from 1 kmol X2 with degree of dissociation α at P atm	KP = (2α)²/(1 − α) × P/(1 + α)
Temperature at which 10% of H2 dissociates at 10 atm?	About 3,535 K (KP = 0.404, ln KP = −0.906, interpolated between 3,400 and 3,600 K)
Why is the real adiabatic flame temperature below the complete-combustion value?	Dissociation of products is endothermic and absorbs part of the released energy; the fragments keep chemical energy until they recombine
How does combustor pressure affect dissociation and flame temperature?	Higher pressure suppresses dissociation (Δν > 0), so the flame temperature is higher
Condition for two phases of a pure substance to be in equilibrium	Equal specific Gibbs functions: g_f = g_g (equivalently h_fg = T s_fg)
Gibbs phase rule	IV = C − PH + 2: independent intensive variables = components − phases + 2
Degrees of freedom of a pure substance in one, two and three phases	2, 1 and 0: a region (T and P free), a curve (P_sat follows from T) and a point (triple point)
Is quality counted as a degree of freedom in the phase rule?	No: it is a ratio of phase amounts, not an intensive property of a phase
Raoult's law	y_i P = x_i P_sat,i(T): vapour partial pressure of i equals its liquid mole fraction times its pure saturation pressure
Henry's law	P_i,gas = H · y_i,liquid, with H (bar) the Henry's constant; y_i,liquid = P_i,gas/H
What does a large Henry's constant mean?	Low solubility: O2 in water has H = 45,000 bar at 300 K, CO2 only 1,710 bar
How does Henry's constant for gases in water change with temperature?	It rises, so solubility falls: warm water holds less O2 and CO2
Before using Henry's law at a free water surface, what correction is needed?	Subtract the water vapour pressure: use the partial pressure of the dry gas, not the total pressure
Mole fraction of air dissolved in lake water at 17 °C under 92 kPa (H = 62,000 bar)?	P_dry air = 92 − 1.94 = 90.06 kPa = 0.9006 bar; y = 0.9006/62,000 = 1.45 × 10⁻⁵
Vapour over a 60% NH3 / 40% H2O liquid at 30 °C by Raoult's law?	About 99.8% NH3 at roughly 702 kPa (P_sat NH3 ≈ 1,167 kPa, P_sat H2O = 4.25 kPa)
For which two-component cases do Raoult's and Henry's laws apply?	Raoult: volatile ideal liquid solutions; Henry: a sparingly soluble gas dilute in a liquid
