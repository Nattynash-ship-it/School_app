What is the stagnation state of a moving gas?	The state it would reach if brought to rest isentropically with no shaft work; properties carry subscript 0 (h0, T0, P0)
Stagnation enthalpy equation and its condition	h0 = h + V²/2 (J/kg); holds for any adiabatic flow with no shaft work, reversible or not
Stagnation temperature of an ideal gas from velocity	T0 = T + V²/(2 cp), with cp in J/kg-K (1,005 for air) and V in m/s
Stagnation pressure from static pressure and temperatures	P0 = P (T0/T)^(k/(k−1)); exponent 3.5 for air; requires an isentropic path
Speed of sound in an ideal gas	c = √(kRT) with R in J/kg-K and T in kelvin; depends on temperature and gas only, not pressure
Speed of sound in air at 300 K	√(1.4 × 287 × 300) = 347 m/s
Definition of Mach number	M = V/c, the local velocity over the local speed of sound at the same static temperature
T0/T, P0/P and ρ0/ρ in terms of M for air	T0/T = 1 + 0.2M², P0/P = (1 + 0.2M²)^3.5, ρ0/ρ = (1 + 0.2M²)^2.5
Which stagnation property is conserved across friction or a shock, and which falls?	T0 (and h0) stay constant in adiabatic flow; P0 falls because entropy rises
Below what Mach number is a gas flow usually treated as incompressible?	About M = 0.3, where the Bernoulli estimate of P0 − P is only about 2% low
Mach number regimes by name	Subsonic M < 1, sonic M = 1, transonic about 0.8 to 1.2, supersonic M > 1, hypersonic M > about 5
Mach angle of a supersonic disturbance cone	μ = arcsin(1/M); 30° at M = 2, 19.5° at M = 3
Area-velocity relation for isentropic duct flow	dA/A = (M² − 1) dV/V
Why can a converging nozzle never exceed M = 1 at its exit?	M = 1 requires dA = 0, a minimum area; supersonic acceleration needs the area to increase after a throat
Critical (starred) ratios for air at M = 1	T*/T0 = 0.8333, P*/P0 = 0.5283, ρ*/ρ0 = 0.6339
General formula for the critical pressure ratio	P*/P0 = [2/(k + 1)]^(k/(k−1)); 0.528 for k = 1.4, 0.546 for k = 1.3
What does it mean for a nozzle to be choked?	M = 1 at the throat; mass flow is at its maximum for that P0, T0 and A* and no longer responds to back pressure
Choked mass flow rate of air	ṁmax = 0.6847 A* P0 / √(R T0), with A* in m², P0 in Pa, R = 287 J/kg-K, T0 in K
How does choked mass flow scale with P0 and T0?	Directly with P0 and inversely with √T0
A/A* at M = 2 for air	1.6875
Why does every A/A* above 1 give two Mach numbers?	One subsonic and one supersonic solution exist for each area ratio; the back pressure decides which occurs
Converging nozzle: exit pressure when Pb is below P*	The exit pressure stays at P*; the extra expansion to Pb happens outside the nozzle in expansion waves
Five back-pressure regimes of a converging-diverging nozzle	Subsonic throughout; choked with subsonic divergence; normal shock inside; overexpanded (oblique shocks outside); design then underexpanded (expansion fans)
Overexpanded versus underexpanded nozzle	Overexpanded: exit pressure below Pb, oblique shocks outside. Underexpanded: exit pressure above Pb, expansion fans outside
Downstream Mach number across a normal shock in air	M2² = (M1² + 5)/(7M1² − 1); general form M2² = (M1² + 2/(k−1))/(2kM1²/(k−1) − 1)
Static pressure ratio across a normal shock	P2/P1 = 1 + (2k/(k+1))(M1² − 1) = (1 + kM1²)/(1 + kM2²)
Density ratio across a normal shock and its limit	ρ2/ρ1 = V1/V2 = (k+1)M1²/(2 + (k−1)M1²); tends to 6 for air as M1 → ∞
Entropy generated across a normal shock	s2 − s1 = cp ln(T2/T1) − R ln(P2/P1) = −R ln(P02/P01) > 0
Normal shock at M1 = 2 in air: M2, P2/P1, T2/T1, P02/P01	M2 = 0.577, P2/P1 = 4.5, T2/T1 = 1.6875, P02/P01 = 0.721
Why can a shock not go from subsonic to supersonic?	Mass, momentum and energy allow it, but the entropy change would be negative, which the second law forbids
What are the Fanno and Rayleigh lines on a T-s or h-s diagram?	Fanno: states satisfying mass and energy; Rayleigh: states satisfying mass and momentum; a normal shock jumps between their intersections
How does an oblique shock relate to a normal shock?	Only the velocity component normal to the wave is shocked, with effective Mach number M1 sin β; the tangential component is unchanged
Weak versus strong oblique shock solutions	Weak: smaller wave angle, downstream usually supersonic, the one nature picks when attached. Strong: larger angle, subsonic downstream
What is a Prandtl-Meyer expansion?	Isentropic turning of supersonic flow around a convex corner through a fan of Mach waves; M rises, P and T fall
Rayleigh flow: definition and effect of heating on M	Frictionless constant-area flow with heat transfer; heating drives M towards 1 from either side (thermal choking at M = 1)
Fanno flow: definition and what stays constant	Adiabatic constant-area flow with friction; T0 is constant, P0 falls, entropy rises, M is driven towards 1
Rayleigh oddity: when does heating air lower its static temperature?	In subsonic flow between M = 1/√k = 0.845 and M = 1, because kinetic energy grows faster than heat is added
Exit velocity of a steam nozzle from an enthalpy drop in kJ/kg	V = 44.72 √(h0 − h) m/s; 44.72 = √2000 converts kJ/kg to J/kg under the root
What is supersaturation in a steam nozzle?	Rapid expansion carries dry vapour below its saturation temperature without condensing; a metastable undercooled state until the Wilson line
Effect of supersaturation on nozzle mass flow and exit velocity	Mass flow slightly higher (denser vapour) and exit velocity slightly lower (smaller enthalpy drop) than equilibrium tables predict
What is the Wilson line?	The locus on the Mollier chart where supersaturated steam suddenly condenses in a condensation shock, releasing latent heat
What does a pitot tube read in a supersonic stream?	The stagnation pressure behind the bow shock in front of it (P02), lower than the free-stream P01; correct it with the shock relations
