Define the COP of a refrigerator	COP_R = Q_L / W_net,in: heat removed from the cold space per unit of work input
Define the COP of a heat pump	COP_HP = Q_H / W_net,in: heat delivered to the warm space per unit of work input
How are COP_HP and COP_R of the same machine related?	COP_HP = COP_R + 1, because Q_H = Q_L + W_net,in for any cycle
One ton of refrigeration in SI units	211 kJ/min = 3.517 kW (12,000 Btu/h); the rate that freezes 1 short ton of water at 0 °C in 24 h
Reversed Carnot refrigerator COP	COP_R,Carnot = T_L / (T_H − T_L), temperatures in kelvin
Reversed Carnot heat pump COP	COP_HP,Carnot = T_H / (T_H − T_L), temperatures in kelvin
Two reasons the reversed Carnot cycle is not built	Compressing a wet liquid-vapour mixture damages compressors; a turbine expanding mostly liquid recovers little work for its cost
Four processes of the ideal vapour-compression cycle	1-2 isentropic compression; 2-3 constant-pressure heat rejection (condenser); 3-4 throttling; 4-1 constant-pressure heat absorption (evaporator)
State 1 of the ideal vapour-compression cycle	Saturated vapour at the evaporator pressure: h1 = h_g, s1 = s_g
State 3 of the ideal vapour-compression cycle	Saturated liquid at the condenser pressure: h3 = h_f
How is h4 found after the throttling valve?	h4 = h3 (isenthalpic throttling); state 4 is a wet mixture at the evaporator pressure
Refrigeration effect per kilogram	q_L = h1 − h4 (kJ/kg)
Compressor work per kilogram in the vapour-compression cycle	w_in = h2 − h1, with h2 from the superheated table at the condenser pressure and s2 = s1
Condenser heat rejection per kilogram	q_H = h2 − h3 = q_L + w_in
Why is the 'ideal' vapour-compression cycle internally irreversible?	The throttling process 3-4 is irreversible, so its COP is below the reversed Carnot COP between the same saturation temperatures
Which process is a vertical line on a P-h diagram?	The throttling 3-4 (constant enthalpy); the condenser and evaporator are horizontal lines
Isentropic efficiency of a compressor	η_C = (h2s − h1) / (h2a − h1): isentropic work over actual work; actual work = isentropic work / η_C
Effect of subcooling the liquid before the valve	Lowers h3 and h4, so q_L = h1 − h4 rises at no cost in compressor work; COP increases
Effect of superheating the vapour before the compressor	Protects the compressor from liquid but raises the specific volume, so work per kg rises and COP falls slightly
How to find the enthalpy of subcooled liquid refrigerant	Use h_f at the liquid's temperature (not its pressure): h3 ≈ h_f@T3
Why keep the evaporator pressure above atmospheric?	So air and moisture cannot leak in; moisture freezes in the valve and air is non-condensable in the condenser
Why must the condensing temperature be well below the critical temperature?	Above the critical point there is no latent heat to reject; CO2 (Tc = 31.0 °C) must run transcritical in warm surroundings
Montreal Protocol (1987) vs Kigali Amendment (2016)	Montreal phased out ozone-depleting CFCs and HCFCs; Kigali phases down high-GWP HFCs such as R-134a and R-410A
ASHRAE safety class of ammonia (R-717)	B2L: higher toxicity, mildly flammable; used in industrial plants, not homes
Normal boiling point of R-134a	About −26.1 °C at 1 atm; ammonia boils at about −33.3 °C
Why does an air-source heat pump's COP fall in cold weather?	T_H − T_L grows, so the ideal COP T_H/(T_H − T_L) falls, and the outdoor coil frosts and needs defrosting
Advantage of a ground-source heat pump	The ground stays near the yearly mean temperature, so the temperature lift is smaller in both seasons and the COP is higher; cost is the buried loop
What does the reversing valve in a heat pump do?	Swaps the roles of the indoor and outdoor coils so the same unit heats in winter and cools in summer
Energy balance on the cascade heat exchanger	ṁ_A (h5 − h8) = ṁ_B (h2 − h3): heat absorbed by the upper loop equals heat rejected by the lower loop
Why does a cascade system beat a single stage for a large temperature span?	Smaller pressure ratio per compressor, cooler discharge, less flash loss in throttling, and each loop can use its own refrigerant
Role of the flash chamber in multistage compression	Liquid is throttled to an intermediate pressure; the flashed vapour goes to the high-pressure compressor, only the liquid continues to the evaporator
Quality after throttling into a flash chamber at intermediate pressure P_i	x = (h_condenser exit − h_f@P_i) / h_fg@P_i
Components that replace the compressor in an absorption system	Absorber, solution pump, generator (with rectifier) and regenerator
Classic refrigerant-absorbent pairs in absorption refrigeration	Ammonia in water (below 0 °C duty) and water in lithium bromide solution (air-conditioning)
COP of an absorption refrigeration system	COP_abs = Q_L / Q_gen (pump work negligible); actual values are usually below 1
Maximum COP of an absorption system	COP_abs,max = (1 − T0/Ts) × T_L/(T0 − T_L): Carnot engine efficiency times Carnot refrigerator COP
COP of the ideal gas (reversed Brayton) refrigeration cycle	COP = 1 / (r_p^((k−1)/k) − 1); depends on the pressure ratio only
Why are gas refrigeration cycles used on aircraft?	Light, simple, open cycle on engine bleed air with no refrigerant; the cold turbine exit air goes straight to the cabin
Seebeck effect vs Peltier effect	Seebeck: a temperature difference across two dissimilar-conductor junctions produces a voltage. Peltier: a current through them pumps heat from one junction to the other
Refrigeration effect in the reversed Brayton cycle	q_L = c_p (T1 − T4), where T4 = T3 / r_p^((k−1)/k) is the turbine exit temperature
