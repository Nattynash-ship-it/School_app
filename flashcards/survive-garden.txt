How many hours of direct sun does a fruiting crop like tomato or pepper need?	Full sun: at least 6 hours of direct sun a day; more is better
Which crop group tolerates less light than fruiting crops?	Leafy and root crops: lettuce, spinach, arugula, chard, radish, herbs
Which crop needs no sun at all?	Sprouts in a jar
Which window direction gets the most sun in New York City?	South-facing; east and west give a few hours; a window facing a taller building may get none
May pots be kept on a fire escape in New York City?	No. The NYC Fire Code (section 1027) requires fire escapes kept clear and unobstructed at all times; enforced by the FDNY
Minimum container size for tomatoes, peppers, eggplant or cucumbers?	At least 8-10 gallons of potting mix, 12-16 inches deep (University of Maryland Extension)
Why not fill pots with garden or street soil?	Garden soil is too heavy for containers and NYC street soil may carry lead; use bagged potting mix
Which crops take up the least lead from contaminated soil?	Fruiting crops (tomatoes, peppers, beans, squash); leafy and root crops take up more
What is the biggest lead exposure from a city garden?	Swallowing soil: children playing in bare dirt and dust tracked into the home on shoes
Three ways to cut lead risk when gardening in NYC ground?	Raised beds of clean soil, cover bare soil with mulch or fabric, wash or peel produce; shoes off at the door
Days from sowing to harvest for radish?	25-35 days (University of Maryland Extension)
Days to harvest for baby arugula and mature arugula?	Baby leaves about 20 days; mature 40-50 days
Days to harvest for spinach?	Baby leaves about 25 days; mature about 40 days
Days to harvest for leaf lettuce?	Baby leaves 25-30 days; mature 30-45 days
What number overrides any days-to-harvest table?	The days-to-maturity printed on your seed packet
When water is scarce, what is the order of use?	Drinking first (at least 1 gallon per person per day), then cooking and hygiene, plants last
What water may go on food plants in a shortage, and what may not?	Clean rinse water from vegetables, rice or pasta may; water with soap, bleach or detergent may not
Which pots do you give up first when water runs short?	The thirsty fruiting crops (tomatoes); keep greens, herbs and sprouts
What is realistic in calories from a windowsill garden?	Almost none; greens give vitamins, freshness and flavour while calories come from the pantry
Which bacteria are linked to raw sprout outbreaks?	E. coli, Salmonella and Listeria; most outbreaks trace to contaminated seed
Does rinsing sprouts thoroughly make them safe?	No. Bacteria can sit under the seed coat where rinsing cannot reach
Who does the FDA advise to avoid raw or lightly cooked sprouts?	Children, older adults, pregnant people and people with weakened immune systems; cook sprouts thoroughly for them
Difference between sprouts and microgreens?	Sprouts: seed germinated in a jar of water, eaten whole, no light. Microgreens: seedlings in a tray of soil under light, cut above the soil
What do you do with a jar of sprouts that smells sour or is slimy?	Throw it out, wash the jar, start again; when in doubt, throw it out
Do microgreens regrow after cutting?	No; sow a new tray every week for a steady supply
How many community gardens are registered with NYC Parks GreenThumb?	More than 550 gardens and farms
How do you reach GreenThumb to be connected with a nearby garden?	Call (212) 602-5300 or email greenthumbinfo@parks.nyc.gov; map at nycgovparks.org/greenthumb/join-a-garden
What must never go in an apartment compost bin, and why?	Meat, dairy, oily or cooked food; an open or smelly bin draws rats in New York. Keep it sealed
USDA hardiness zone for New York City on the 2023 map?	Zone 7b, all five boroughs; average winter low 5 to 10 degrees F
Average last spring frost date for New York City?	Early April, about April 3 (Cornell Cooperative Extension Harvest NY; Urban Farmer)
Average first fall frost date for New York City?	About October 20
How long is the outdoor growing season in New York City?	Roughly 6.5 months, early April to mid-October
Name cool-season crops that survive light frost.	Lettuce, spinach, arugula, radish, kale, chard, peas, scallions, cilantro, parsley
Name warm-season crops killed by frost.	Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, beans, basil
Last realistic outdoor sowing date for radish in NYC, and how you get it?	About mid-September: count back 25-35 days from the October 20 average first frost
What is an open-pollinated variety and why save seed only from it?	It breeds true, so saved seed grows the same plant; F1 hybrid seed grows a random mix of its parents
Which four vegetables are the easiest to save seed from?	Beans, peas, lettuce and tomatoes, because they mostly self-pollinate
How do you test old seed before you trust it?	Germination test: ten seeds on a damp paper towel in a bag for about a week; buy fresh if few sprout
First two steps against aphids on an indoor food plant?	Squash or wipe them off by hand, or rinse them off with a firm spray of water; check under leaves every few days
Rule for any pesticide used on a plant you will eat?	Only a product whose label says it is for edible plants, used exactly per label; the label is the law
Fix for fungus gnats in a pot?	Let the top of the soil dry out between waterings and use yellow sticky traps
What is the limiting factor for growing indoors in a New York winter?	Light, not temperature; sprouts, microgreens, scallions and slow herbs are what works
