List the four air-standard assumptions	Air as an ideal gas in a closed loop; all processes internally reversible; combustion replaced by external heat addition; exhaust replaced by heat rejection to the initial state
What does the cold-air-standard add to the air-standard assumptions?	Constant specific heats at 25 °C values: cp = 1.005 kJ/kg·K, cv = 0.718 kJ/kg·K, k = 1.4
Define the compression ratio r of a piston engine	r = V_max/V_min = V_BDC/V_TDC, a ratio of volumes (not pressures)
Clearance volume from displacement and compression ratio	V_c = V_d/(r − 1), because r = (V_d + V_c)/V_c
What is the mean effective pressure (MEP)?	The constant pressure that acting over the whole stroke would give the same net work: W_net = MEP × (V_max − V_min)
Engine power from work per cycle	Ẇ = W_cycle × n_cyl × N/n_R, with N in rev/s and n_R = 2 for four-stroke, 1 for two-stroke
Why is the Carnot cycle not used for real gas engines?	Isothermal heat transfer is too slow, it needs very large pressure and volume ratios, and its MEP is low
Which two averages control the efficiency of any cycle?	Average temperature of heat addition (raise it) and average temperature of heat rejection (lower it)
SI versus CI engine	Spark ignition: mixture lit by a spark plug (Otto cycle). Compression ignition: air compressed until fuel injected into it self-ignites (Diesel cycle)
The four processes of the ideal Otto cycle	1→2 isentropic compression; 2→3 constant-volume heat addition; 3→4 isentropic expansion; 4→1 constant-volume heat rejection
Cold-air-standard Otto efficiency	η = 1 − 1/r^(k−1); depends only on compression ratio r and k
Isentropic temperature and pressure ratios for a compression ratio r	T₂/T₁ = r^(k−1); P₂/P₁ = r^k
What limits the compression ratio of spark-ignition engines?	Autoignition of the compressed air-fuel mixture, heard as engine knock
How does the ideal Diesel cycle differ from the Otto cycle?	Heat is added at constant pressure (2→3) instead of constant volume; q_in = c_p(T₃ − T₂)
Define the cutoff ratio	r_c = v₃/v₂, cylinder volume after combustion over volume before it (Diesel cycle)
Cold-air-standard Diesel efficiency	η = 1 − (1/r^(k−1))·(r_c^k − 1)/(k(r_c − 1))
Otto or Diesel: which is more efficient at the same compression ratio?	Otto; the Diesel bracket is above 1. Diesels win in practice by running much higher compression ratios
What is the dual cycle?	Heat added partly at constant volume (pressure ratio r_p) and partly at constant pressure (cutoff ratio r_c); Otto and Diesel are its limits
Ideal Otto, r = 8, cold air: efficiency?	1 − 1/8^0.4 = 56.5%
The four processes of the ideal Brayton cycle	1→2 isentropic compression; 2→3 constant-pressure heat addition; 3→4 isentropic expansion; 4→1 constant-pressure heat rejection
Cold-air-standard Brayton efficiency	η = 1 − 1/r_p^((k−1)/k), with r_p the compressor pressure ratio
Define the back-work ratio	bwr = w_comp,in/w_turb,out, the fraction of turbine work used to drive the compressor
Pressure ratio for maximum Brayton net work	r_p = (T₃/T₁)^(k/(2(k−1))); then T₂ = T₄ = √(T₁T₃) and w_max = c_p(√T₃ − √T₁)²
Actual compressor and turbine exit temperatures from isentropic efficiencies	T₂a = T₁ + (T₂s − T₁)/η_C; T₄a = T₃ − η_T(T₃ − T₄s)
Regenerator effectiveness	ε = (T₅ − T₂)/(T₄ − T₂): actual preheat over the maximum possible (up to turbine exhaust temperature)
When does regeneration help a gas turbine?	Only when the turbine exhaust is hotter than the compressor exit (T₄ > T₂): low pressure ratio, high turbine inlet temperature
Ideal Brayton efficiency with ideal regeneration	η = 1 − (T₁/T₃)·r_p^((k−1)/k); it falls as r_p rises
Best stage pressure ratio for two-stage compression with perfect intercooling	√r_p for each stage, which splits the work equally
Effect of intercooling and reheating without regeneration	Net work up, back-work ratio down, thermal efficiency usually down
Five components of a turbojet, in flow order	Diffuser, compressor, combustor, turbine, nozzle
In an ideal turbojet, what sets the turbine work?	It equals the compressor work (plus small accessories); the useful output is the jet kinetic energy
Thrust of a jet engine (exit at ambient pressure)	F = ṁ(V_exit − V_inlet)
Propulsive power	Ẇ_P = F·V_aircraft = ṁ(V_exit − V_inlet)V_aircraft
Propulsive efficiency as defined by Cengel & Boles	η_P = Ẇ_P/Q̇_in, propulsive power over rate of fuel energy input (many propulsion texts call this the overall efficiency)
Froude propulsive efficiency	η = 2/(1 + V_exit/V): highest when the jet is only a little faster than the aircraft
Ram temperature rise in an ideal diffuser	ΔT = V²/(2c_p) with c_p in J/kg·K
Bypass ratio of a turbofan	Mass flow of air bypassing the core divided by mass flow through the combustor
What does an afterburner do?	Burns extra fuel between turbine and nozzle to raise the nozzle inlet temperature, jet velocity and thrust, at high fuel cost
Why can a ramjet not take off on its own?	Its only compression is ram compression in the diffuser, which needs high flight speed
Processes of the Stirling cycle	Isothermal expansion at T_H, constant-volume regeneration, isothermal compression at T_L, constant-volume regeneration
Why do Stirling and Ericsson cycles reach Carnot efficiency?	All external heat transfer is isothermal at T_H and T_L; the rest is exchanged internally by a perfect regenerator
