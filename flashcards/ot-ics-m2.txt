What is a threat actor?	The person or group behind an attack
On which THREE attributes does SY0-701 describe threat actors?	Internal vs external; resources/funding; level of sophistication/capability
Which threat actor is external, very well resourced, very sophisticated and patient — often called an APT?	Nation-state
What does APT stand for?	Advanced Persistent Threat — a well-resourced, patient attacker, usually a nation-state
Which threat actor is motivated primarily by financial gain and runs like a business (ransomware, affiliates)?	Organised crime
Which threat actor is motivated by philosophical or political beliefs and wants attention?	Hacktivist
Which threat actor is the only one with legitimate access?	Insider threat
Does 'insider threat' require malice?	No — it includes careless or well-meaning employees who cause harm unintentionally
Which 'threat actor' on the exam list is not an adversary at all?	Shadow IT — employees using unapproved systems, which creates undefended attack surface
Which threat actor has low resources and low skill and uses others' tools?	Unskilled attacker (older material: script kiddie)
Name the 'taking' motivations on the SY0-701 list.	Data exfiltration, espionage, financial gain, blackmail
Name the 'breaking' motivations on the SY0-701 list.	Service disruption, disruption/chaos, war
Name the 'believing' motivations on the SY0-701 list.	Philosophical/political beliefs, ethical, revenge
A penetration tester with written permission is driven by which listed motivation?	Ethical — same techniques as a criminal, distinguished by authorisation
What is a threat vector?	The path an attack takes to reach you
What is an attack surface?	The sum of all points where an attacker could get in
Which vector carried Stuxnet across an air gap?	Removable device (USB media)
Client-based vs agentless vulnerability scanning — the difference?	Client-based installs an agent on the host; agentless scans over the network with nothing installed
Why is agentless scanning often the only option in OT?	Installing software on a controller is usually forbidden or impossible
What is a supply chain vector?	Compromise through a vendor, supplier or service provider (e.g. an MSP) whose access or code you inherit
What does MSP stand for?	Managed Service Provider — an outside company that runs part of your IT, with matching access
What was Colonial Pipeline's reported initial vector?	A legacy VPN account without multi-factor authentication
Phishing vs smishing vs vishing?	Email (or general messaging) vs SMS text vs voice call
What is business email compromise (BEC)?	A real or convincingly spoofed executive/partner account used to request a payment or data transfer
Pretexting vs impersonation — the distinction?	Impersonation is WHO the attacker claims to be; pretexting is the invented STORY that makes the request seem legitimate
What is a watering-hole attack?	Compromising a website the targets already visit, then waiting for them
What is typosquatting?	Registering a domain one keystroke from a real one (rnicrosoft.com)
Misinformation vs disinformation?	False content spread accidentally (mis-) vs deliberately (dis-)
What is brand impersonation?	Pretending to be a trusted company — its logo, its login page
Ukraine 2015: actor, motivation, vector in, vector across?	Nation-state; service disruption in wartime; spear-phishing with a macro document; stolen credentials on the utility's own remote access
Maroochy 2000: actor and vector?	Insider (disgruntled ex-contractor, revenge); unsecure wireless with a stolen radio
Why does knowing the threat actor matter for defence?	It tells you which door they will use — a nation-state finds doors you did not know you had; organised crime uses the one you left unlocked
