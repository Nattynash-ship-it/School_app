What is a thermal energy reservoir?	A body with heat capacity so large it can supply or absorb finite heat with no measurable change in temperature (a source supplies, a sink absorbs)
Four defining features of a heat engine	Receives heat from a high-T source, converts part to work, rejects the rest to a low-T sink, operates on a cycle
Thermal efficiency of a heat engine (two forms)	η_th = W_net,out / Q_H = 1 − Q_L / Q_H
Net work of a cyclic heat engine in terms of the heat flows	W_net,out = Q_H − Q_L (all three as positive magnitudes)
Kelvin-Planck statement of the second law	No device operating on a cycle can receive heat from a single reservoir and produce a net amount of work
Why can the condenser of a steam plant not be removed?	Compressing the turbine exhaust vapour back to boiler pressure would consume nearly all the turbine work; condensing first makes the return cheap
Is work-to-heat conversion limited by the second law?	No; work converts to heat completely. It is heat-to-work that needs a heat engine and is limited
Steam plant: 280 MW in, 165 MW rejected. Net power and efficiency?	W = 115 MW; η = 115/280 = 41.1%
Coefficient of performance of a refrigerator	COP_R = Q_L / W_net,in = Q_L / (Q_H − Q_L)
Coefficient of performance of a heat pump	COP_HP = Q_H / W_net,in = Q_H / (Q_H − Q_L)
Relation between COP_HP and COP_R for the same machine	COP_HP = COP_R + 1
Why can a heat pump deliver more heat than the electricity it uses?	Q_H = Q_L + W: it moves heat from the cold outdoor reservoir and adds the compressor work; nothing is created
Lowest possible COP of a heat pump in principle	1, the resistance-heater floor: all the work becomes heat even if no heat is lifted from outside
Clausius statement of the second law	No cyclic device can produce no effect other than the transfer of heat from a lower-temperature body to a higher-temperature body
How is the equivalence of the Kelvin-Planck and Clausius statements shown?	Pair a device violating one with an ordinary engine or refrigerator; the combination violates the other, so each implies the other
Perpetual-motion machine of the first kind (PMM1)	A device that violates the first law by producing energy from nothing (energy out exceeds energy in)
Perpetual-motion machine of the second kind (PMM2)	A device that conserves energy but violates the second law, e.g. an engine with a single reservoir or a work-free refrigerator
Refrigerator removing 360 kJ/min with a 2 kW compressor: COP_R and Q_H?	Q_L = 6 kW, COP_R = 3.0, Q_H = 8 kW
What is a reversible process?	One that can be reversed leaving no trace on the surroundings: system and surroundings both return to their initial states
Name four irreversibilities	Friction, unrestrained expansion, heat transfer across a finite temperature difference, mixing (also electric resistance, inelastic deformation, chemical reaction)
Internally versus externally reversible	Internally: no irreversibilities inside the system boundary (quasi-equilibrium). Externally: none outside, i.e. heat transfer across zero temperature difference
When is heat transfer reversible?	Only in the limit of an infinitesimal temperature difference between the bodies
Four processes of the Carnot cycle	1-2 reversible isothermal expansion at T_H (Q_H in); 2-3 isentropic expansion; 3-4 reversible isothermal compression at T_L (Q_L out); 4-1 isentropic compression
Shape of the Carnot cycle on a T-s diagram	A rectangle: horizontal isotherms at T_H and T_L, vertical isentropic legs; enclosed area = net work, area under the top edge = q_H
Heat added on the isothermal leg of an ideal-gas Carnot cycle	q_H = R T_H ln(v₂/v₁) (Δu = 0, so heat in equals work out)
Volume ratio across an isentropic leg of an ideal gas	v₃/v₂ = (T_H/T_L)^(1/(k−1)); for air with k = 1.4 the exponent is 2.5
First Carnot principle	An irreversible heat engine is always less efficient than a reversible one operating between the same two reservoirs
Second Carnot principle	All reversible heat engines operating between the same two reservoirs have the same efficiency
Why do the adiabatic legs of an ideal-gas Carnot cycle contribute no net work?	The expansion leg does +c_v(T_H − T_L) and the compression leg −c_v(T_H − T_L); they cancel exactly
Definition of the thermodynamic temperature scale	(Q_H/Q_L)_rev = T_H/T_L for a reversible engine between the reservoirs; the unit fixed by the triple point of water at 273.16 K
Carnot efficiency	η_th,rev = 1 − T_L/T_H with both temperatures in kelvin
Carnot COP of a refrigerator	COP_R,rev = T_L/(T_H − T_L) = 1/(T_H/T_L − 1)
Carnot COP of a heat pump	COP_HP,rev = T_H/(T_H − T_L) = 1/(1 − T_L/T_H)
Carnot engine between 652 °C and 30 °C: efficiency?	1 − 303.15/925.15 = 0.672 (67.2%); using Celsius would wrongly give 0.954
Max COP of a heat pump keeping 21 °C with outdoor air at −5 °C	294.15/26 = 11.3
How do you judge a claimed engine efficiency or COP?	Compute the Carnot ceiling from the kelvin temperatures: above it is impossible, equal means reversible, below is a real machine
What does 'quality of energy' mean?	The same quantity of heat yields more work when available at a higher temperature; work is the highest-quality form
Work obtainable from 100 kJ of heat at 1000 K vs 400 K with a 300 K sink	70 kJ at 1000 K (η = 0.70) versus 25 kJ at 400 K (η = 0.25)
How does the Carnot efficiency change with T_H and T_L?	Rises as T_H increases, rises as T_L decreases; only the ratio T_L/T_H matters
How do Carnot COPs change as the temperature lift widens?	Both COP_R,rev and COP_HP,rev fall as T_H − T_L grows; the smallest lift gives the highest ceiling
Why is electric resistance heating thermodynamically wasteful?	It degrades high-quality work into low-temperature heat that a heat pump could deliver for a fraction of the electricity
