Why is a Carnot vapour cycle impractical (three reasons)?	Heat addition capped at T_crit (373.95 °C); wet turbine expansion erodes blades; compressing a two-phase mixture to exactly saturated liquid is not feasible
Name the four processes of the ideal Rankine cycle	1-2 isentropic pump; 2-3 constant-pressure heat addition in boiler; 3-4 isentropic turbine expansion; 4-1 constant-pressure heat rejection in condenser
Pump work formula for the ideal Rankine cycle	w_pump = v_1 (P_2 − P_1), with v_1 = v_f at the condenser pressure in m³/kg and P in kPa, giving kJ/kg
Rankine heat input and heat rejection per kilogram	q_in = h_3 − h_2 (boiler); q_out = h_4 − h_1 (condenser)
Rankine thermal efficiency in terms of heats	η_th = w_net/q_in = 1 − q_out/q_in, with w_net = w_turb − w_pump = q_in − q_out
Typical back work ratio of a steam Rankine plant	Under 1% (about 0.4% in the 3 MPa/75 kPa example), versus 40-80% for a Brayton gas turbine
Why is Rankine pump work so much smaller than Brayton compressor work?	Steady-flow work is ∫v dP; liquid water has v ≈ 0.001 m³/kg, about a thousand times smaller than the gas in a compressor
How to find the turbine exit state in an ideal Rankine cycle	Set s_4 = s_3 at the condenser pressure; if s_f < s_4 < s_g then x_4 = (s_4 − s_f)/s_fg and h_4 = h_f + x_4 h_fg
Efficiency of the 3 MPa, 350 °C, 75 kPa ideal Rankine example	26.0% (q_in = 2728.6 kJ/kg, q_out = 2018.6 kJ/kg, x_4 = 0.886), Cengel & Boles tables
Rankine efficiency in terms of average temperatures	η_th = 1 − T_L,avg / T_H,avg, both entropy-averaged absolute temperatures in kelvin
Effect of lowering the condenser pressure	Raises efficiency (lower T_L,avg) but lowers turbine exit quality and needs a bigger condenser; floor set by cooling-water temperature
Effect of superheating the steam	Raises T_H,avg and raises exit quality; limited by turbine metallurgy to about 620 °C
Effect of raising the boiler pressure at fixed inlet temperature	Raises T_H,avg and efficiency but lowers exit quality (more moisture); modern plants reach 30 MPa supercritical
Practical limit on turbine exhaust moisture	About 10% moisture (quality 0.90 or higher) to protect the blades from droplet erosion
What does reheat do and why is it used?	Expand in an HP turbine, reheat at constant pressure in the boiler, expand again; used mainly to keep the exhaust dry at high boiler pressure, with about a 4-5% efficiency gain in modern plants
Heat input and turbine work in the ideal reheat cycle	q_in = (h_3 − h_2) + (h_5 − h_4); w_turb = (h_3 − h_4) + (h_5 − h_6); q_out = h_6 − h_1
Typical optimum reheat pressure	About one-quarter of the boiler pressure (4 MPa for a 15 MPa boiler)
Result of the 15 MPa/600 °C reheat-at-4 MPa example	η_th = 45.0%, exit quality 0.896 (10.4% moisture), q_in = 3896.1 kJ/kg, versus 43.0% and 19.6% moisture without reheat
Water saturation properties at 10 kPa (Cengel & Boles A-5)	T_sat = 45.81 °C, v_f = 0.001010 m³/kg, h_f = 191.81, h_fg = 2392.1 kJ/kg, s_f = 0.6492, s_fg = 7.4996 kJ/kg·K
Why does regeneration raise Rankine efficiency?	Extracted steam does the low-temperature feedwater heating instead of fuel, so the average temperature of external heat addition rises
Define an open feedwater heater	A direct-contact mixing chamber where extracted steam and pumped condensate mix and leave as saturated liquid at the extraction pressure; needs a pump downstream
Define a closed feedwater heater	A shell-and-tube exchanger: extracted steam condenses on tubes carrying feedwater at boiler pressure; the drain is pumped forward or throttled back
Extraction fraction for one open feedwater heater	y = (h_3 − h_2)/(h_6 − h_2): heater exit minus incoming feedwater, over extracted steam minus incoming feedwater
Extraction fraction for a closed feedwater heater	y = (h_fw,out − h_fw,in)/(h_ext − h_drain), with h_drain = h_f at the extraction pressure
Which quantities are weighted by (1 − y) in a one-heater regenerative cycle?	Condenser heat (h_7 − h_1), LP turbine work (h_6 − h_7) and pump I work; boiler heat, HP turbine work and pump II use the full kilogram
Result of the 15 MPa/600 °C open-FWH-at-1.2 MPa example	y = 0.227, q_in = 2769.1 kJ/kg, q_out = 1486.9 kJ/kg, η_th = 46.3% (versus 43.0% without regeneration)
Trade-off of regeneration	Higher efficiency but lower net work per kilogram of boiler steam, so a larger steam flow is needed for the same power
Open versus closed feedwater heaters	Open: simple, cheap, effective, deaerates, but one pump per heater. Closed: no extra pump, one HP feedwater line, but costlier and less effective
What happens to a closed-heater drain in a trap?	Throttling: constant enthalpy, pressure falls, a small fraction flashes to vapour, entropy rises
Isentropic efficiency of a turbine	η_T = w_actual / w_isentropic = (h_3 − h_4a)/(h_3 − h_4s); actual work is smaller, so multiply the ideal work by η_T
Isentropic efficiency of a pump	η_P = w_isentropic / w_actual = (h_2s − h_1)/(h_2a − h_1); actual work is larger, so divide the ideal work by η_P
Typical isentropic efficiencies of large steam turbines and feed pumps	Turbines 0.85-0.92; feed pumps 0.75-0.85
Three ways a real Rankine cycle departs from the ideal	Pressure drops from friction in boiler, condenser and piping; heat loss to surroundings; irreversible (non-isentropic) turbine and pump
Effect of turbine irreversibility on the exhaust state	Higher entropy and enthalpy at the same condenser pressure, so the exhaust is drier than the isentropic case
Result of the 8 MPa/500 °C, 10 kPa actual-cycle example (η_T 0.85, η_P 0.80)	w_turb = 1078.8 kJ/kg, w_pump = 10.09 kJ/kg, w_net = 1068.7 kJ/kg, η_th = 33.4% versus 39.4% ideal
Define the utilisation factor of a cogeneration plant	ε_u = (W_net + Q_process)/Q_in = 1 − Q_condenser/Q_in; it counts both electricity and process heat as useful
What is cogeneration?	Producing electricity and process heat from the same fuel by expanding steam through a turbine and then using it (extracted or exhausted) for heating
Combined-cycle efficiency with perfect heat recovery	η_cc = η_gas + η_steam − η_gas × η_steam (e.g. 0.38 + 0.30 − 0.114 = 0.566)
Why is a combined gas-vapour cycle so efficient?	Heat is added at gas-turbine temperatures (1400-1600 °C) and rejected at steam-condenser temperatures (30-45 °C); best plants reach about 60%
What is an HRSG?	Heat recovery steam generator: the boiler of a combined cycle, heated by gas-turbine exhaust instead of a flame
Key energy balance of an HRSG	m_gas × c_p × (T_exhaust,in − T_stack) = m_steam × (h_steam,out − h_feedwater,in), which fixes the steam-to-gas flow ratio
What is a binary vapour cycle?	Two Rankine cycles with different fluids coupled by a heat exchanger: a high-critical-temperature topping fluid (historically mercury) condenses by boiling water in the bottoming cycle
Why is water a poor topping-cycle fluid?	Its critical temperature is only 373.95 °C, so adding heat at high temperature requires very high pressures
